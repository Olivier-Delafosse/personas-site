## Two ways of borrowing a voice

For decades, brands that wanted an authoritative editorial voice had one option: hire a ghostwriter. A human with editorial instincts, who could carry the brand's positions across articles, talks, social posts, board memos.

In 2026, brands have a second option: rent an AI persona.

The comparison is messier than either side wants to admit. Let's lay it out honestly.

## Where the AI persona wins

**Cost.** A persona license costs a fraction of a senior ghostwriter's monthly rate. For a small brand, that's the difference between having an editorial voice and not having one.

**Throughput.** A persona doesn't get tired. If you need fifteen posts this week, the persona writes fifteen posts. It doesn't push back on the deadline.

**Consistency at the line level.** A well-designed persona keeps its voice across every output. A human ghostwriter is consistent over time, but every individual piece reflects their mood that morning, their argument with their partner, their second coffee.

**Availability.** It's 11pm. The board memo is due tomorrow. The persona responds.

**Disclosure.** AI personas can be disclosed as AI. Human ghostwriters cannot be disclosed as ghostwriters without giving up the entire point of the arrangement. Some buyers want the "AI, declared" version specifically because it sidesteps a question that ghostwriting has never fully answered.

## Where the human ghostwriter wins

**Judgement.** A good ghostwriter knows when not to write the thing. They'll push back on a take that will age badly, that's factually shaky, that's politically tone-deaf, that doesn't actually serve the brand. The AI persona will execute. The human will sometimes refuse.

**Sources of taste.** Ghostwriters carry years of reading, conversations, fights, and recoveries from things that aged badly. That taste is encoded in their writing in ways that don't appear in a prompt. An AI persona has been trained on a frozen slice of the world. A ghostwriter is updating their model in real time, off lunch with a sharper friend.

**Relationship.** A ghostwriter develops the voice across months of conversations with the principal. They learn what the principal actually believes, what they say they believe, and the gap between the two. They write the version that's coherent with the principal's deeper position, not just the surface ask. The AI persona has to be told.

**Original framing.** A good ghostwriter will sometimes invent a frame. A new way of thinking about an old problem. AI personas can recombine known frames, but they don't generate the next one. That's a real difference.

## Where both fail in the same way

Both can produce content that's well-formed and wrong. Both can flatter the principal into a position that will embarrass them later. Both can become the voice of the principal so thoroughly that the principal stops being able to hear their own actual voice when they need to.

Both can be used as a cover for not thinking carefully about what to say.

## The honest decision matrix

For a brand owner deciding between the two, or between renting both, the question is not "which is better". It's:

**What is the cost of getting it wrong on a given piece?**

For social posts, newsletters, recurring industry briefings, the cost of getting it wrong is low. A misfired post is annoying, not catastrophic. AI personas are well-matched to this surface.

For board memos, regulatory filings, public statements during a crisis, fundraising decks, the cost of getting it wrong is high. The judgement of a ghostwriter who has watched four other companies fumble this exact moment is worth several times what they cost. AI personas are not currently well-matched to this surface.

For the middle band, analyst reports, considered op-eds, long-form thought pieces, it depends on whether the company has a strong enough internal review process to catch a well-formed wrong thing. If the only person reviewing the persona's output is the principal who asked for it, the persona will eventually slip something past them. If there's a sharp editor in the loop, the persona is cheaper than the ghostwriter on this band too.
